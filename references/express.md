# Express mode (the default)

Express makes a 30–90 s film in **20 minutes of work at most** and **no stop after the Brief**: the agent passes every gate itself (`state.mjs approve <n> --by agent`) and hands over the finished film. It is the default answer to Brief Call 1's "Mode"; Studio (three.js 3D, reference boards, benchmark, reviewer) is the other answer. `BRIEF.md` says `mode: express`. Every rule of SKILL.md still holds unless this file says otherwise: words first, every change on a word, facts sourced, true shapes, free tools.

| Minute | Work | Who |
|---|---|---|
| 0–2 | preflight (background) and the topic question at once, folder, one Brief call | main |
| 2–5 | facts (one round, ≤ 2 research agents at once, one per independent strand, each ≤ 6 searches and ≤ 8 fetches, 4 min) → `script.md` + `narration.txt` → Gate 1 (agent) | research subagent(s), main |
| 5–12 | voice take, and at the same time the build | background job + the builder(s) |
| 12–16 | build finishes, lints | builder(s) |
| 16–20 | one render at `--quality looks` → Gate 4 (agent) → the film to the user | main |

Over budget at a step: say so with the clock time, cut scope (fewer shots, shorter film), never skip a fact check or the timing.

## Express Steps 3–4 — Script and voice

- Script as in `script-voice.md` Step 3 (beats `{text|name}`, provenance tags, `numbers_lint.mjs`, Promise and Takeaway, `narration_lint.mjs`, the read-through), at most 8 beats. No quiz, no arc template look-ups. The research brief: one round, one agent per independent strand (at most 2, started together), each ≤ 6 searches and ≤ 8 page fetches, original sources first; an unsourced number comes back `[unknown]`. A topic that rests on health, law, standards or measured numbers is where one thin round costs a second, sequential one: name the claim the film turns on in each brief (what the numbers or rules are, who sets them), so the budget goes to primary sources. A second round only for a core claim still `[unknown]`, started the moment the first returns.
- Approve Gate 1 yourself once `numbers_lint.mjs`, `narration_lint.mjs` and the read-through are clean (`state.mjs approve 1 --by agent`), then in one message: start the full take in the background (`tts_timestamps.mjs … --retake auto`, the voice from `BRIEF.md`; `--sample` only when the user has not heard this voice before), write `SPEC.md` (LOOK, MOTION, OBJECTS: 3–6 lines each, BANNED) and `storyboard.md` (one row per beat: beat name, what is on screen, the overlay text), and start the builder. The builder needs beat names, not times: every time is read from `audio/beats.js` at runtime, so it writes the whole template at once and never waits for the take (below).
- Gate 2 is the agent's: approve it (`state.mjs approve 2 --by agent`) when the take ends with no WARNING left; a sentence still flagged after `--retake auto` is respelled once (`--retake changed`), then named in the hand-off.

## Express Step 5 — Look in one frame

- One builder (a new one, model and effort from `SKILL.md`'s table: mid, medium) writes the whole film; **from 6 beats or 45 s, 2–3 builders write it at once** (below). No asset search (one model query at most, below), no reference board, no style-frame check, no benchmark.
- **The look is an editorial infographic in one design language, `isometric-world` first** (EpicInfographics, MIT, vendored in `<skill-dir>/engine/epic/`; Studio uses the same languages in three.js, `looks.md`). Pick: `isometric-world` by default, every topic; `cutaway` when the film is about the inside of one thing (how a machine, a body or a building works inside: same isometric view, paper palette, ink lines, numbered callouts); `blueprint` only when the film rests on very detailed data (many exact measures, dimensions, specs or tolerances the viewer must read). One language per film, picked from the subject without a question and named in `SPEC.md` LOOK; a language the user names wins. The builder reads that `languages/<name>.md` plus `composition.md`, `illustration-and-texture.md` and `data-vocabulary.md`, and treats the language's palette, fonts, geometry and signature devices as law. `SPEC.md` LOOK names the language, the composition pattern and the metaphor.
  - **The frame is a place, not a page:** before drawing, say where the viewer stands ("above a model town", "beside the machine on its slab, its casing slid off"); background, texture and furniture belong to that place, with dense small detail and a story path (route, flight line, beam) that links the beats. Type and shapes on a plain ground is slop.
  - **One object carries the data:** a drawn object of the subject whose parts hold the numbers (fill level, rings, rungs, segments), labels pinned to its parts with leader lines; one hero per beat, 2–4 support forms of different kinds, never three of the same chart.
  - **Each beat's last frame is a finished infographic:** one named composition pattern (Big Object, Bleed, Overlap stack, Diagonal drive, Editorial spread, Specimen sheet), one dense zone and one empty, three sizes at least (biggest ≥ 8× body), something crossing a boundary. Truthful geometry and the hard rules of `charts.md` (area scales with value, no dual axis, ≤ 5 donut segments, text never in the data colour, numbers formatted for people, no emoji as icons).
- **`isometric-world` and `cutaway` are drawn with `engine/iso/iso.js`** (grammar after lemo-opuscar's `iso-infographic`, MIT; Canvas 2D, no three.js): the subject as a small diorama on a floating board with a cut edge, on a pale ground (`cutaway`: on paper, a `skin` slid off, ink lines and numbered callouts); charts, type and furniture are inline SVG and HTML over it. `blueprint` is inline SVG and HTML: the subject in near-white linework on blueprint blue, drawn in the same isometric view (a plan or elevation of one part only as an inset), dimension lines with real values, a title block.
  - **True isometric, never perspective:** axes at 30°, the projection never tilts or rotates; the camera only pans and zooms (zoom interpolated in log).
  - **Three tones per face, no outlines:** top lightest, left mid, right darkest, all from one base colour (`tri()`); solids are told apart by tone alone.
  - **Colour is a legend:** a pale ground, 5–7 hues, one ink; one hue per material or role for the whole film; the subject's hue is reserved, and to point at it everything else greys out (focus + context).
  - **Infographic furniture lives in the world:** label pins (dot → leader → rule → text) that never cover the subject, dashed cut lines, hatched section faces, a route line, a "you are here" ring, a circular inset for a close-up.
  - **Numbers are drawn, not said:** Isotype rows of identical icons that add up to the figure on screen.
  - **True shapes still:** each object built from its real parts and proportions; a machine gets its tell-tale parts, never a generic block. A cutaway shows the real inside.
  - **Objects are data, not drawing code** (`engine/iso/build.js`, README there): each object is a `build()` spec written from its reference, parts of shape families at real size in metres (`box`, `cyl`, `lathe` for anything turned, `extrude` for a footprint or outline, `sweep` for pipes and roads, `gear`, `wheel`, `board`, `figure`), with `array` copies, a `skin` for the cutaway, `focus`, and numbers named as `values` the timeline drives; pins come from its `at`. A complex common object (furniture, a vehicle, a tool) may instead be one CC0 model: one `find_media.mjs --type model` query, ≤ 4000 triangles, taken into `assets/models/` and drawn by a `model` part.
- **The builder never idles on the voice.** It writes `src/index.tpl.html` and the film's timeline from beat names first, with no `until [ -f audio/beats.js ]` or sleep loop before the first write. Only the steps that need real times wait: `fill_times.mjs`, the snapshot, the lints. When `audio/beats.js` lands (or changes after a retake) it reruns `fill_times.mjs` and checks. The builder polls the file once between jobs, never as its work.
- The builder's first picture, once `audio/beats.js` exists, is one frame: `npx hyperframes snapshot --at <the hook beat's seconds> --no-end --describe false -o style-frames/`. The builder looks at it once against the anti-slop list (cover the words: is the topic still clear? could this layout hold any other data? cards in a grid on a flat ground? emoji? everything 16–40 px with no giant anchor?) and recomposes on any hit. The main agent approves Gate 3 itself (`state.mjs approve 3 --by agent`, Express needs no benchmark) and the builder continues; it never waits for the user.

## Express Step 6 — Build (one builder)

Builder brief: `BRIEF.md`, `SPEC.md`, `storyboard.md`, `narration.txt`, the `/hyperframes` skill, this section and Step 5's look. Return ≤ 10 lines. **Another video's folder is off limits** (`video/<other-slug>/`: its `index.html`, `beats.js`, script, assets): the pattern comes from `engine/`, the design language files and the `/hyperframes` skill, so no earlier film's look, copy or numbers leak in.

- One composition (`src/index.tpl.html` → `fill_times.mjs` → `index.html`) and one GSAP timeline built from `BEATS`/`WORDS` only, so a seek renders the same frame every time. The furniture (type, charts, pins) is inline SVG and HTML the timeline tweens (never CSS keyframes, never `requestAnimationFrame`). The world: `cp <skill-dir>/engine/iso/*.js assets/` (API: `<skill-dir>/engine/iso/README.md` and the comment block atop each file), one full-frame `<canvas>` under the SVG furniture; a model is `await loadModel(...)` before the timeline is built; the timeline holds the animated values and its `onUpdate` redraws the canvas (`iso.cam`, then `build()` per object and the furniture, then `iso.flush()`).
- **Build order per beat, on the word:** the place is already there (never a blank first frame) → structure (title, axes, the object's outline, 0.4–0.8 s) → data (bars grow by `scaleY`, arcs sweep, paths draw, fills rise, icon armies populate) → the hero lands last with the biggest beat while nothing else moves → support text. Arrivals decelerate (`power3.out`); `isometric-world` and `cutaway` may overshoot a little, `blueprint` never bounces. **Truthful motion:** comparable marks share one duration and ease (stagger the delays), a count-up ends exactly on the value, an axis never moves once data sits on it.
- **Motion has a cause, led by a line:** a route or leader line draws a little ahead of what moves; a cutaway is cut line (~0.3 s) → near skin slides out along its normal (~0.35 s, ease-out) → hatched rims. Solids arrive by moving in, never by fading (a see-through block reads as a ghost); landing squash ~18 % and a small bounce; a fall eases in. Isotype icons pop with an overshoot, one per beat subdivision, the count-up number in step. Every shot keeps one continuous motion.
- Camera moves from the menu that fits the story: follow a route (cause → effect), hold wide with an inset, push through nested cutaways, a fast pull-out to the whole infographic, a locked frame while things pile up, a pan down through strata (the view never tilts). The subject ≥ 1/3 of frame height at its key moments.
- **Depth by parallax** (`engine/iso/parallax.js`): 3–4 layers at most, a far one (0.2–0.5: sky, distant skyline, paper behind), the subject (1) and a near one (1.3–1.6: a leaf, a machine edge). The world: `drawLayers(iso, cam, rest, layers)`, each layer its own pan and zoom, never a tilt; SVG/HTML furniture: one wrapper `<g>` or `<div>` per layer with `data-depth`, and `applyLayers(root, view, rest, W, H)` in the timeline's `onUpdate`. Layers move only when the camera does; a near layer crossing the lens is the occlusion wipe between beats.
- **Shot like a film** (`engine/iso/cinema.js`, README there): pick 4–6 techniques in `SPEC.md` CAMERA, each with its story job (`camera-motion.md` §11–12), never all. The camera moves between keyed poses (`camAt`: eased, zoom in log), with `handheld` life rising with tension; `drawLayers` options give depth of field (`focus` racked on the beat word from subject to detail) and `haze` (far layers paler); `ramp` slows story time into the impact; `whip` + `motionBlur` (16–24 samples) cut between places; `makeFinish(...).apply` once per frame, last: a grade per section (warm or cool, contrast), bloom on what glows, a light sweep on a reveal, vignette, grain 0.04–0.06, and scope bars (2.39) eased in for the establishing shot or the climax only.
- Text: the language's fonts and size scale, scaled to the frame (its scale is for a 1080 px canvas; set `FONT` in `assets/iso.js` for canvas text), big numbers with half-size units; captions from `WORDS` in the language's caption style at the bottom (keep that zone clear).
- Pitfalls: give every face an outward hint (else it is culled), draw a composite as one ordered item, leave clearance between a wheel and the floor, skip small detail when zoomed in close.
- Audio: the narration; music and SFX only from the bundled `/media-use` library (`find_media.mjs --type sfx --bundled`), light and acoustic, ducked under the voice. No web search for media.
- Checks before returning: `npx hyperframes lint`, `node <skill-dir>/scripts/lint_scene.mjs`, `node <skill-dir>/scripts/timing_lint.mjs`; fix what they report.
- Log the build: `state.mjs log "6 Build | index.html | <problems or ->"`.

### Parallel build (6 or more beats, or 45 s or more)

Build time is the longest serial step, so a longer film is written by 2–3 builders in one message. Under that size one builder is faster than the hand-offs.

- **Groups:** cut the beats into 2–3 runs of neighbouring beats with about equal runtime. `SPEC.md` is the contract all of them share (language, palette hex, fonts, easing, caption style, the camera techniques); the main agent writes it before any builder starts, so there is no pilot.
- **Each group is a sub-composition** (`look-build.md` "Parallel build" item 2: `src/compositions/<group>.tpl.html` → `compositions/<group>.html`, local times, group-prefixed ids). A builder touches only its own template, never `src/index.tpl.html`, the audio or another group. Brief each with the other groups' beat names, so a seam is planned: one group ends on its held last frame, the next opens in the same place and ground colour.
- **The first group's builder also writes `src/index.tpl.html`:** hosts for every group by name, the captions and the audio. When all report, the main agent runs `fill_times.mjs` on the template and each group, then `hyperframes lint`, `lint_scene.mjs` and `timing_lint.mjs`; a failure goes back to that group's builder only, replaced by a new one a notch up (`SKILL.md` "Escalate").
- One seam check, not a review: a single snapshot at the last frame of each group and the first of the next, read by a mid-tier, low-effort subagent that answers "same place, same palette, nothing jumps?" per seam.

## Express Step 7 — Render → GATE 4

- One render: `npx hyperframes render --fps <brief fps> --quality looks --quiet`, then `npx hyperframes check` on it. No 10 fps draft, no strips, no reviewer, no quiz.
- Approve Gate 4 yourself (`state.mjs approve 4 --by agent`) once `check` passes, and send the film. A change the user asks for afterwards goes to a new builder with the list; render again.
- `--quality high` only when the user asks for it. Hand-off: `POST.md` and two lines in `PRODUCTION.md` (time spent, tokens from `review/tokens.json`).

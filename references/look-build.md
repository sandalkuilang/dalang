# Steps 5–6: look, storyboard, style frames, build

## Step 5 — Look, storyboard, style frames → GATE 3

Read the chosen look's row in `looks.md`; `visual-direction.md` §1, the chosen energy's row in §3, §5–8, §12; `craft-rules.md` §2–6; and `camera-motion.md` §1–4, §8, §10 and §11. Write:

- `SPEC.md` (template: `/hyperframes` `references/spec.md`): the LOOK line (the chosen look and energy in numbers — palette hex, faces and weights, light direction, grain, average shot length), the MOTION and ANCHORS lines (`camera-motion.md` §8), OBJECTS (each physical thing, its asset rung and source, and the real reference it is checked against), BANNED. Never skip it: without it every gap falls back to the generated default look.
- `storyboard.md`: the arc, a colour-script line and the axis per section (`camera-motion.md` §3), then the shot table, times from `beats.json`:

| # | Time | Beat | Job · emotion | Size · angle · lens | What we see (focal point; fg / mid / bg job) | Camera (+ what changed) | Subject action (+ source) | Transition out (+ what fires it) | SFX (J/L) | Overlay (headline · cards · labels) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 0.00–1.60 | `hook` | A · establish | macro · low · 100 mm | the hero mid-action (focal); its context blurred mid | push 1.00→1.04 | the hero's key action happens on the hook word (stock clip) | speed ramp into the cut on the next beat word | the action's sound, J −0.3 s | kicker + headline: the hook's claim as a takeaway; card A: its value counting |

Check: every row names its emotion and its size, angle, lens, move, light and colour follow `camera-motion.md` §12 or the SPEC rulebook — a choice without a motivation is cut, a rule break is logged; every row's "Subject action" names a real action and its source — footage, three.js 3D, a `/hyperframes-registry` block (`catalog --query`, in English) or a composited physical animation — and camera-only rows are ≤ 15 % of the runtime; every "why" beat has its mechanism shot (§8 "Motion is the explanation"); the Job column marks A or B roll and the A/B pattern follows `camera-motion.md` §10; every perceptible camera move has a real source (footage, 3D, or a generated clip only on paid request), never CSS on a still; one wow shot on the impact; each row's Camera column names its technique from the SPEC palette (`camera-motion.md` §11), and the light ratio follows the colour script. Every OBJECTS item that is a physical thing has a real source (rungs 1–5) — a vector stand-in is a blocker, not a compromise; every shot where the subject acts has footage or 3D for it (§1 "Moving subjects"), and every person who moves or speaks has real footage (§1 "People"; a generated clip only on paid request); every beat visualises its claim, not just its words; the Overlay column, written now and not per scene in Step 6, names which layer carries which fact (`visual-direction.md` §12): a headline per claim beat, at most 3 cards, sides and hues fixed, no fact in two layers, and the headlines read alone tell the argument; continuous motion in every row; at Calm energy few hard cuts; the hero meets `visual-direction.md` §7's size; one hero move per shot; text in the format's safe zone and in the narration language; stylised characters (only if asked for) follow the mitten rule; a reference's numbers are used (Step 2). Shot grammar (`camera-motion.md`): arc named, Crack and Impact present; every row has a job; every perceptible camera move has a reason; each scene keeps its axis; sizes tighten toward the key beat and macro inserts have their own asset; a pause before the biggest cut and no two bursts touching; the final image is the payoff frame.

Then run `node <skill-dir>/scripts/storyboard_lint.mjs`: exit 1 names the row (empty Subject action or Camera, 3+ rows alike, too much text, shots too long, `visual-direction.md` §3); fix the table, log the result. `state.mjs` refuses to log Step 6 before `storyboard.md` exists and Gate 3 is approved.

Then get the hero assets (resolve, ingest, cut out every OBJECTS item; free sources first: `find_media.mjs --type image --query "<1–2 English words>" --out review/<dir>` searches Wikimedia Commons then Openverse for CC0 / CC BY / public-domain stills, downloads previews and prints one line each). Queue every still in one background chain, style-frame ones first (SKILL.md "Batch and parallelise"). A product photo on a white backdrop (a press-kit shot, or a still generated on a white light box when the user asked for one): key it with `<python with Pillow + numpy> -I <skill-dir>/scripts/key_white.py in.png out.png [--holes]` (`--holes` clears backdrop seen through a handle; off when the object has a white label) — `hyperframes remove-background` is a person-segmentation model and punches holes in products, and a green screen keys dark bodies. **Every file that lands in `assets/`** — images, clips, narration, music, SFX — goes into `ASSETS.json` at once (file, source URL or "generated"/"user", licence, author; model + prompt + seed for generated ones — format in `scripts/check_assets.mjs`). Free stills, music and SFX come from `node <skill-dir>/scripts/find_media.mjs` (below): its `--take` writes the row from the API's own licence and author, never typed by hand. A file you derive from a taken one (a fitted track, a keyed cutout) is written over the taken path, or it is a new file that needs its own row with the same source, licence and author. The engine's files (`build.mjs` copies about 25 into `assets/`) are never rows: the build lists them itself in `.engine.json`, with the origin and licence of each vendored package, and `check_assets.mjs` reads that. The narration's licence is the output licence of the model that spoke (the `Model` line of `tts_timestamps.mjs`), never the app's code licence; `check_assets.mjs` cross-checks the row against `audio/*.response.json` and fails a non-commercial voice listed under a commercial licence.

**Style frames** (the look test; a change costs three frames now, every scene later): a small composition in `style-frames/src/` with the real assets and rig — the hook scene at its real timing from 0 s with the narration `<audio>`, then the hardest action shot (the mechanism, the 3D or footage clip, ideally the wow shot), one B-roll with its real move, and the payoff as ~3 s clips — the look test must prove the motion, not only the stills. Load only the `/hyperframes` contract and `references/format.md` (plus `types/vertical.md` for 9:16), use the scaffold in §6. Snapshot the three at their mid points (`npx hyperframes snapshot --at <t1>,<t2>,<t3> --describe false`) into `style-frames/`, and render it once as the **moving sample** (`render --fps <fps from BRIEF.md> --quality high --quiet --output style-frames/sample.mp4`, then `ffmpeg -t <hook end> -i style-frames/sample.mp4 -c copy style-frames/hook.mp4`) — stills can't show motion, rhythm or how the voice sits. A subagent compares the three stills with `SPEC.md` and the OBJECTS references and returns a text verdict; the stills stay out of this context. Step 6 seeds its first template from these scenes. Build nothing else before Gate 3. Run the asset pass (`review.md`) on the stills so far. Show `storyboard.md`, `SPEC.md`, the three stills and the hook sample together and **wait for approval**.

## Step 6 — Build (hand off to /hyperframes)

1. Load `/hyperframes` and only the type guides this video needs (`types/explainer.md`, `types/character.md`, `types/data.md`, `types/3d.md`, plus `types/vertical.md` for 9:16). Answer its opening questions from `BRIEF.md`, `script.md`, `storyboard.md`, `SPEC.md` without re-asking the user — but **do not skip its design steps**: it follows the LOOK, OBJECTS and BANNED lines.
2. Style frames are the reference per scene; 3+ groups: "Parallel build".
3. Search the catalog before building an effect; queries **in English** (others return nothing): `npx hyperframes catalog --query "<what the beat needs>"`, then `npx hyperframes add <name>`.
4. Load `audio/beats.js`; **every** animation time references `BEATS.<name>.start`, or `WORDS[i].start` for a change on a word that is not a beat. Never hard-code seconds or numbers (`script-voice.md` "Numbers model"). HTML attributes (`data-start`, `data-duration`) can't read JS: write `{{beat_name - 0.05}}` in the template (`src/index.tpl.html`; a group's own: "Parallel build") and run `node <skill-dir>/scripts/fill_times.mjs <template> <output>` after every template edit and new take. Captions and labels on a three.js canvas: run `node <skill-dir>/scripts/captions.mjs audio/narration.txt audio/words.json` after every take, load `audio/captions.js` (`CAPTIONS`) and draw with the engine's `type.js` — `drawCaption(ctx, t, CAPTIONS, { format, accent })` once the frame is on the 2D canvas, `pinLabel` for a label pinned to a part — then check `repeats(CAPTIONS, LABELS)` prints nothing. Headlines and cards come from the storyboard's Overlay column (`visual-direction.md` §12), in the text band above the caption, each timed from `BEATS`/`WORDS`; add their texts (with `from`/`to`) to the list given to `repeats()`. Never write per-film caption code.
   Scaffold facts, each a lint round when missed: include GSAP yourself (`<script src="https://cdn.jsdelivr.net/npm/gsap@3.14.2/dist/gsap.min.js"></script>`, before your timeline script); time a `<video>` **or** its wrapper, never both (a timed scene wrapper holds an untimed `<video>`, or the reverse); `media-treatment` adds `data-hf-id` to every tag, so edit the template with Edit, not `sed`; never put SVG inside a `<template>` (it renders empty), never `mix-blend-mode` on an SVG `<rect>` (it paints opaque); set every face with `font-family` (the font subsetter skips the `font:` shorthand, and the render falls back to a system font); the same hero across a cut is a match cut, never a crossfade. Three more, each a full draft render: every plate drifts from its first version (overscan 1.06–1.15, ~30 px/s, a yoyo on long holds; static plates measured 27 % still); prefix every `id` with its scene (`hk-`, `calc-`; a shared id moved an object in another scene); a whip pan moves the outgoing and incoming plates in the same window, each overscanned, so no empty frame shows.
5. **Blocking pass (animatic):** first the asset pass (`review.md`) on the stills made since Gate 3; then every scene at final timing with rough motion. Render once at `--fps 10 --quality draft --quiet`, watch it against the narration — rhythm, shot variety, colour script — and run `stillness.mjs` on it now: too little motion is a structure problem (`visual-direction.md` §3 "Minimum motion"), cheap to fix here.
6. **Polish pass:** craft (`visual-direction.md` §4; physical timing, compositing and design in time from `camera-motion.md` §5–7), finish (§9: contact shadows, one light direction, grade and grain through `media-treatment`), depth and parallax (§6). Captions sit at the target position of `craft-rules.md` §6 (lower third on 9:16), checked on the phone sheet.
7. Audio: first normalise the narration (`loudness.mjs audio/narration.wav --fix <project>/assets/narration.m4a`) so the mix lands near −14 LUFS; music ducking under it (`/media-use` `references/mixing.md`; tempo from the energy); physical sound effects come from the bake (`sfx.js`, `camera-motion.md` §5.3), others on key beats; each `<audio>` needs `data-duration` (else it plays to the film's end). SFX sources, in order: the bundled `/media-use` library (`node <skill-dir>/scripts/find_media.mjs --type sfx --bundled --query "<English words>" --out review/sfx`; it reads the library's own `CREDITS.md` for the licence and warns, never guesses, when it cannot), then Openverse CC0 (`find_media.mjs --type sfx --query "<English words>" --licence cc0 --out review/sfx`; add `--max-duration 3` for a short hit); `media-use resolve --type sfx` has no free provider. Music is a licensed free track, not a generated one: the user's own track first, then Openverse, **CC0 first** (`find_media.mjs --type music --query "<1–2 English mood words>" --licence cc0 --out review/music`, no key; most CC0 music is Freesound, which has no category, so music is kept apart from SFX by length: `--min-duration 20` by default), and only when no CC0 track fits, CC BY (`--licence by`, mostly Jamendo), then Wikimedia Commons audio by hand. The script backs off on a rate limit by itself, drops NC and ND results and a CC BY with no creator to credit, and a source that does not answer is a WARN to rerun, never "nothing found". Listen to 2–3 candidates whose mood and tempo fit the energy and offer them at Gate 2; a CC BY track is offered with a plain note that it needs a visible credit, and the user picks where it goes (video description, end card) or picks a CC0 track instead; the upload caption you draft never carries a credit or licence line (no `🎵 Music: … (CC BY)`) unless the user chose one, and a CC0 track needs none; never an NC, ND or SA licence (the script leaves SA out unless `--licence by-sa` asks). `find_media.mjs --take review/music/<file> --as assets/music.mp3` copies the pick into the project and appends its `ASSETS.json` row (source, licence, author from the API; `CREDITS.md` follows from `check_assets.mjs`). Fit it to the film **over the taken path**, so the row still names the file that ships (a fitted copy under another name has no row and `check_assets.mjs` fails it): `ffmpeg -stream_loop -1 -i assets/music.mp3 -t <film length> -af "afade=t=in:d=1,afade=t=out:st=<length − 2>:d=2" -c:a libmp3lame -q:a 2 assets/music.fit.mp3 && mv assets/music.fit.mp3 assets/music.mp3`, then duck it under the narration. Its level is measured: `audio_qc.mjs` flags a bed over 25 dB or under 8 dB below the voice (another name: `--music <id>` per bed). Nothing fits → deliver narration + SFX and say so.
8. `npx hyperframes lint` after each major section, and `node <skill-dir>/scripts/lint_scene.mjs` after every patch and before every snapshot or draft render. A `//` comment goes at the true end of a line, never between two statements: written mid-line it turns the rest of the line into comment text and `node --check` still passes (a scale, shadow or `add` silently dropped; a comma in it merges two list entries). Exit 1 names `file:line` and the swallowed code.

### Parallel build: pilot, then shot groups (Step 6, item 2)

For 3+ shot groups. Fewer: items 3–8 in the main context.

1. **Group the storyboard rows.** Rows that share one physics bake or one continuous camera move are one group, owned by one agent; every other split is by scene or chapter. Name each group (`intro`, `stage-3`).
2. **Each group is a HyperFrames sub-composition.** Template `src/compositions/<group>.tpl.html`, `fill_times.mjs` into `compositions/<group>.html`. The host in `index.html`, the scene root and the `window.__timelines` key share the group id; every element id carries the group prefix; `data-start` and `data-duration` are local to the file (`{{beat - first}}`, `BEATS.x.start - G0`, with `first` the group's first beat and `G0 = BEATS.<first>.start`); `drawCaption` takes absolute narration time, so give it `t + G0`. The other format rules are in `/hyperframes` `references/format.md`.
3. **Pilot.** Build the first group in the main context through items 3–6. Render it as a 10 fps draft with only its host mounted (one render, so heavy jobs stay one at a time), and have a subagent check it against the style frames. Its files are the pattern for every other group.
4. **Groups in parallel.** One subagent per other group, all started in one message, on the strongest model (its code is the film). Each gets `SPEC.md`, its storyboard rows, the pilot's files (read-only) and the set modules; it follows items 3–6 and 8 with item 4 read for its group (its own template and file only, local times `{{beat - first}}`; it never edits `src/index.tpl.html`, the audio or another group's files, which the main context and the other subagents write), checks `npx hyperframes snapshot --composition <group>` against the style frames, and returns file paths and a verdict, never code.
5. **Assemble.** In the main context only: the hosts in `src/index.tpl.html` (`data-composition-src="compositions/<group>.html"`, `data-start="{{first}}"`), their order, the audio (item 7); then the blocking pass (item 5) on the whole film. Read a group's code only when its check failed.
6. **Step 7 sees the groups.** `timing_lint.mjs` (no argument) follows each host into its `compositions/<group>.html` and adds the host's start to the group's local times, so a cut inside a word or a clip under 1 s inside a group is reported (with the group file and the local time to fix); a group file it cannot read or that still holds `{{…}}` is exit 1. Run it on `index.html` only, never on a group file (local times against absolute word times give wrong verdicts); `score_run.mjs` counts the same.

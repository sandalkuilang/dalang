# Looks (Step 1 options; Step 5 reads only the chosen row)

Each look is a medium with numbers, not an adjective. A look decides everything around the subject (set, light, type, graphics, rhythm); the subject follows the realism chosen in Step 1 (`visual-direction.md` §1): A, B, E, F are hyperreal; C, G, I, J are stylized; D and H carry either. Write the chosen one as the LOOK line of `SPEC.md`. "Feels like" names a genre so the user can picture it; it is never a licence to copy anyone's characters, frames, or brand.

| Look | Feels like | Use for | The medium in numbers |
|---|---|---|---|
| **A. Cinematic photoreal** (default for realism) | premium product film, high-end documentary B-roll | products, vehicles, places, food, comparisons | Real hero (rung 1–5) on a lit set. Key light upper left, rim light opposite, contact shadow 0.35–0.55 opacity with 24–40 px blur, background plate blurred 8–16 px. Grain 3–5 %, vignette 6–10 %, one grade through `media-treatment`. Palette: two colours sampled from the hero plus one accent. Data as thin overlays on the object. |
| **B. Premium 3D studio** | product launch keynote, tech reveal | one object as the payoff, mechanisms, cutaways | One object on a seamless sweep, physically based materials, roughness ≥ 0.45. Turn the environment or light, not the object. One camera dive per section. |
| **C. Studio diorama** (stylized; the most-loved three.js look) | a designed miniature object, product-design render, cozy diorama | mechanisms, devices, how things work, places as miniatures | The subject as a designed object on a **light, warm set** by default (off-white walls, a light warm-grey floor or seamless sweep, e.g. #f4efe9 walls and #dcd8d0 floor: examples, not fixed values) with a soft ground shadow, seen from an elevated three-quarter view with a long lens (~50 mm). **Light set, dark detail:** the hero (often light itself) separates from the ground by **value**, through its dark functional parts (grilles, vents, handles, cables, trim, screws), N8AO and a contact shadow, never by darkening or saturating the ground. Rule: **at least ~0.2 OKLCH L** between the hero's silhouette (or its dark trim) and the ground right behind it (`node <skill-dir>/scripts/palette.mjs --compare <hero> <ground>`); a light hero on a light ground of the same value merges. Accents are small and few, each with a job (heat, current, cold, the part that matters), one per shot (`visual-direction.md` §5 Restraint). A dark or saturated full-frame ground only when the user or a measured reference asks: a mid-grey ground reads sleepy, a saturated one garish. Rounded edges (bevel ≥ 4 % of size), matte paint, soft even studio light, small believable details (vents, screws, seams, labels). Saturated candy pastels and coloured backdrops read cheap. Characters follow the mitten rule (`craft-rules.md` §5) and act with squash ≤ 8 %. Built in three.js (recipe below) or from a stylised GLB (generated stylised stills only on paid request). |
| **D. Editorial kinetic type** | modern essay channel, campaign film | opinion, myths, lists, quotes, muted viewing | Type carries the film. Display face at 10–14 % of frame height on 9:16, tracking −2 % to −4 %, one accent word per line. Photos appear as cutouts. Cuts on the beat. |
| **E. Documentary collage** | investigative explainer, history channel | history, news, geography, "how did we get here" | Real archive photos and maps as paper cutouts with torn edges and 2–4 px offset shadows, halftone on photos, tape and marker accents, camera moving across a desk-like plane. Every photo is credited. |
| **F. Real subject + motion graphics** | science explainer channel, infographic film | systems, biology, space, how things work | The subject as real footage or photos (`visual-direction.md` §1); the invisible parts (forces, flows, axes, labels, cross-sections) as vector graphics **with light** over it: one shade and one highlight tone per colour, soft radial gradients, grain 2–4 %, 3 parallax layers. Graphics track the subject, never replace it. A 3D motion (a roll, a twist) needs the viewing axis that shows it, or an end-view inset. |
| **G. Ink + watercolour** (stylized) | hand-made storybook | stories, history, gentle topics | `references/craft-rules.md` (boil, palette). |
| **I. Toon explainer** (stylized) | premium animated explainer, cel-shaded 3D | how things work, mechanisms, science, processes | Toon 3D in three.js: 3 hard tone steps, a dark ink outline, flat palette (one cool ground, one warm and one cool hero colour), crisp shadow, no grain. Recipe below. |
| **J. Engineering cutaway** (stylized; technical illustration) | engineering explainer, teardown film | how a made thing works, why it breaks, what is inside it | A real engineered thing **opened** (cut along its axis or exploded), every part at real size, printed markings legible in the macro (a pump, a lock, a gearbox, a phone, a valve). World: a neutral light-grey ground (e.g. #e8e9e6) and a working surface that gives scale (a grey-blue mat with a 10 mm grid), never a void. Lens: 36 mm gauge; 70 mm wide, 100 mm medium, 135 mm macro; 3/4 high angle (elevation 20-60°); the subject a little low (view offset ≈ -4.5 % H) so the text band sits on clean ground over a light scrim. Light: soft key high front-left, cool fill, warm rim; shadow box refitted per station, AO radius ≈ framed radius / 12, AgX, grain 0.035, bloom only above luminance 1.0 (current, LEDs, arcs); hard-surface materials (masked board with clearcoat, tape, ferrite, tinned metal, moulded plastic with a fine grain). Macro: DOF on the subject (bokeh ≈ 6, focus range ≈ 0.6 × framed radius). Text band (`textBand`): kicker in monospace caps, tracked, 36 px, over a bold headline with the claim or number (≈ 76 px), a dark status tag. Labels: ≤ 3 per frame, yellow pills on a dark leader ending in a ringed dot (`pinLabel` `pill`). Caption: dark pill, the key word in a yellow box. Accents mean one thing for the whole film: yellow labels and AC, blue DC, data or cold, orange heat, red warning. Current (`circuit.js` `flow`) is drawn in the world and a nearer part occludes it; **dimension lines** (`quantity.js` `dimension`) are drawn on the frame, on top of the parts, so nothing hides them: dimension an edge no other part stands in front of. Reference piece: `engine/three/examples/torch.js` (`frames()`: wide, medium with flow, macro with DOF). |
| **H. Dimensional data** | data journalism, finance explainer | number-heavy topics | Charts built in reading order (`/hyperframes` `references/types/data.md`) with light and depth: soft shadows, one key light. Numbers as physical comparisons where possible (a stack, a distance, a scale). Never clip-art bars. |

**Mixing:** A may combine with D, F or H (a real hero with kinetic type, explanatory graphics or data overlays). E may combine with D and H. Never put a stylised subject (C, G, I, J) next to a photoreal one.

**Offering them in Step 1:** pick up to 4 looks of the chosen realism that fit the topic — hyperreal from A, B, D, E, F, H (A first for a physical subject), stylized from C, J, I, G, D, H (C first for a thing or a place: the most pleasant to watch; J first for how a made thing works, why it breaks or what is inside it; I when line clarity matters more); for an idea with no physical subject, D and H first in either; name the rest in the question text so "Other" can reach them. Give each option a `preview`: a 6–8 line text sketch of one frame of *this* topic in that look (what is in frame, light, type, motion, the ground colour and how the hero separates from it: its dark parts, its shadow; `visual-direction.md` §11 Figure-ground), so the user chooses with the topic in mind. A preview is a promise: nothing from `visual-direction.md` §5 "Banned" and no void ground (`visual-direction.md` §9), or `SPEC.md` will have to break it.

## three.js recipes (verified: a 3 s 1080p24 shot renders in under 30 s on a 16 GB M5)

### Install the engine into the project

Install the DALANG engine into the project (`node <skill-dir>/engine/three/build.mjs assets`, then `node <skill-dir>/engine/three/selfcheck.mjs assets`; `<skill-dir>/engine/three/README.md`; the build lists every file it copied, with its real origin and licence, in `assets/.engine.json`, and `check_assets.mjs` reads that, so the engine never goes into `ASSETS.json` by hand): one local ESM bundle of three and its add-ons plus Rapier, so every module shares one `THREE`, and the proven systems for everything below (scale, surfaces, settling, physics, cloth, air, fur, plants, particles, granular fills, water, section views, finish; ready pieces from earlier sets in `examples/`). Build on them. The engine holds what earlier films proved; it is not the limit of what a film can show. A subject it has nothing for (a cell's chemistry, a solar system, an organ, a market, an idea) is built the same way: find the law that drives it and its real numbers in a cited source; model it in real units from three.js primitives (instanced meshes, lines, shaders), or show it in a look that needs no 3D (F's graphics over real footage, H's data, D's type); when reality is too small, large, fast or slow to see, change one scale on purpose and show it (`camera-motion.md` §5, "Any subject moves by its own law"); self-check it against those numbers and prove it in a render; then bring it back to the engine as a generic system (its README, "how it grows"). Shaders compile on the first frames and timers are frozen in the capture browser: never wait on a timer in a build step.

### Light and material per look

- **Hyperreal (B, or a rung-5 hero in A):** a real CC0 HDRI as `scene.environment`, its `environmentRotation` chosen from a sweep of 6–12 angles so a softbox edge crosses the hero as the camera moves (a flat grey face reads as plastic); a soft key spot with a PCF shadow (radius 4) and a cool rim; N8AO (spatial, deterministic); DOF gentle enough to soften the backdrop only (strong DOF ghosts edges); AgX tone mapping; vignette and seeded grain drawn in 2D over the frame. Materials carry the realism: different finishes for different machining (a turned face shinier than its cut flanks; one mirror finish everywhere reads as glass), plated fasteners with chamfers, a backdrop kept dark and low-contrast. Path tracing (three-gpu-pathtracer) was not reliable in the capture browser: skip it.
- **Studio diorama (C).** The best-loved three.js scenes are Blender models with lighting baked into one texture; without Blender, approximate the bake: `RoomEnvironment` through PMREM as the environment (intensity ~0.9: soft, even studio light), `MeshStandardMaterial` roughness ~0.55, metalness 0, each part's sides a step darker; parts thicker and rounded (extrude bevel thickness ~15 % of depth, 5 segments; widen meshing parts' spacing by twice the bevel growth); a mount or housing with small details; a `ShadowMaterial` ground (opacity ~0.22) under one soft directional (PCF radius ~12); N8AO (radius ~0.6); neutral tone mapping; no DOF; grain ≤ 0.12 and a vignette near the ground colour. The ground is light and warm (row C); the dark parts, N8AO and the `ShadowMaterial` contact shadow carry the separation: check ΔL with `palette.mjs --compare` on a style frame. A real bake (Blender, Cycles) is richer still — bounce light, glowing practicals — and works only for parts that do not move relative to each other.
- **Stylized (I):** `MeshToonMaterial` with a 3-texel ramp (nearest filtering); an ink outline as an inverted hull (`BackSide`, pushed out ~1 % of the object's size along normals smoothed with `mergeVertices`, so hard edges don't crack the line); no tone mapping, so the picked colours stay; key light plus hemisphere fill, shadow radius ≤ 2; no grain.
- **Engineering cutaway (J):** a light-grey ground with a gridded mat; the example's `lights()` (key, fill, rim) until a studio rig lands; AgX tone mapping, N8AO radius ≈ framed radius / 12, grain 0.035, `BloomEffect` threshold 1.0 (only current, LEDs and arcs pass it); DOF on the macro station only (`frames().macro.dof` into `DepthOfFieldEffect`); hard-surface materials, the assembly cut with `cutaway.js` (below).

### Set dressing, real scale, interior style, condition

- **Set dressing (every 3D set; it decides whether C reads as real or as a toy).** The things around the subject, at their real density, carry the realism more than any light or material: a room sparser than a real one reads as a toy, cartoon or not; space stays nearly empty, the inside of a cell or a battery is crowded. Dress every surface with what would really be there in this place and show it was used (a desk: papers, a mug, cables to an outlet; walls: a pinboard, a clock, a switch; a lab bench: glassware, labels, a notebook; a workshop: offcuts, tools, sawdust; outdoors: leaf litter, pebbles, a puddle), plants with real leaf shapes (a `ShapeGeometry` blade, cupped, on stems; trailing vines) rather than cones, and, where life or machines are present, two or three small motions besides the hero, at their real amount (a breathing pet, a clock's tick, a blinking cursor, rising steam, bubbles in a beaker; still air indoors, steady LEDs). Anything that falls, rolls or is knocked is simulated (`camera-motion.md` §5). Surfaces get canvas textures (plank seams and grain, a rug pattern, screen content, a view out of the window). CC0 kits give the props cheaply (for interiors Kenney Furniture Kit GLB and Quaternius furniture OBJ: `examples/interior.js` `kits.furniture` with a palette; other kits get their own name maps): recolour them by material name into the film's palette, re-shade with `toCreasedNormals` at ~32°, place by footprint centre (`model(path, { at: "footprint" })`; a kit whose origins sit where the piece stands may use them), and light the dressed set with a `CubeCamera` probe rendered once from its centre as `scene.environment` (bounce light without a bake). Work out pixels per real metre from the framing (frame width in px ÷ metres visible across it; a three-quarter view of a 4 m room in 1080p ≈ 150 px/m): a detail under ~6 px reads as noise, so build it only for close-ups. Keep the props in `assets/*.js` modules so the scene file stays under the lint's ~400 lines. A small light patch (sun through a small window) is lost behind props and spill: give a light a clear path or skip it.
- **Real scale first** (`kit.js` `makeKit({ mpu })`). Measure the set's metres per unit from things of known size (a desk top 0.75 m, a door 2.03 m, a car 4.5 m, a test tube 15 cm — kit models are rarely 1 unit = 1 m). Every new prop is modelled in metres and scaled by 1/M at its real size (an adult cat curled ≈ 37 cm, a hardcover 16 × 24 cm, an AA cell 50 × 14 mm, a 16-inch fan); physics runs in real units (the place's gravity over M units/s², 9.81 m/s² on Earth; speeds and drag converted), or everything falls too fast and the set reads as a miniature. Reports read in real mm and m/s.
- **Interior style (only for a set with rooms or furniture; from `BRIEF.md` `interior`; default American-Japandi).** Japanese calm with American home motifs: honey bamboo furniture (laminated strips with growth nodes) and light oak floors, warm white walls, a navy or white Shaker panel door with a white casing and brass knob, oatmeal linen upholstery, navy and a little rust as accents; cushions in ticking stripe, navy linen and buffalo check, a braided cotton rug, a rice-paper lamp shade, an ensō or a quiet print, a ceramic pot. Patterns are drawn as yarn (each band of threads, a twill or weft line), not flat paint.
- **Condition (from `BRIEF.md` `condition`; any made thing: a room, a machine, a street).** `lived-in` (default): sound walls (roller stipple and faint mottling), things in use; `new`: no traces of use; `worn`: flaking paint at damp spots and edges, scuffs where hands and feet go, grime low on walls. Never age a set the topic did not ask to be old.

### True shapes and true surfaces

- **True shapes and true surfaces (every 3D set, stylized too: the shape and the stuff it is made of are real, the palette and framing may be stylized). Always, whatever the subject; each rule applies where its thing is in the set:**
  - **No facets, no blocks.** Low-poly kit models read as toy blocks. Soft and organic things (upholstery, cushions, leaves, a stuffed toy) get two Loop subdivisions (`three-subdivide`, MIT, coplanar faces split first so flat faces stay flat); round hard things (pots, vases) one; hard furniture none — a Loop pass melts a desk into clay, its crisp corners are what make it read as wood. Primitives at 64 segments, rounded boxes at 5.
  - **Every surface has its material's detail**, as tileable map pairs baked from seeded noise (albedo multiplier + under-layer mask, normal + roughness), laid by triplanar projection so no UVs are needed: walls of paint over plaster with a roller stipple (flaking where damp rises, each flake's edge curling with a line of dirt in the break, grime low down — only at the brief's `worn` condition); plain-weave cloth (1 mm threads, slub, fuzz) with `MeshPhysicalMaterial` sheen on upholstery, cushions and curtains; a cotton loop pile on rugs; fine, nearly straight wood grain (~4 mm lines — wide wavy rings read as a contour map); faint smudges in the gloss of everything else. Fixed set pieces project in world space (one tile spans neighbouring wall panels), moving bodies in their own space (the texture travels with them). `material.clone()` drops `onBeforeCompile`: copy it across.
  - **Soft things are stuffed and give under weight.** A cushion is fat in the middle and thin at its seam (a finely divided box, its thickness scaled by the distance from the edge — a rounded box's flat faces are single quads that cannot bulge); a pouf is a sewn drum with piping, its top domed and pressed down where something lies on it. Loose soft things (cushions, a dropped coat) are not placed: they are dynamic bodies in the physics pre-roll and fall and lean against what is there, at rest before frame 0.
  - **Every prop rests on its own lowest points.** Drop it by the smallest gap under its lowest vertex in each cell of a 6 × 6 grid over its footprint (legs, corners, a leaning edge), so it lands on its first contact; a point below a surface means it was placed into it (lift out up to 3 cm, deeper is reported). One ray under the centre drops a desk between its legs through the floor and leaves a leaning cushion hanging on a back edge. Fur and whiskers are not supports; boxes from the vertices, not a rotated mesh's own box.
  - **Animals are built from their anatomy and wear fur.** Sweep each part along its centre line with its real girth (spine, haunch, chest, neck, skull with cheeks and whisker pads, ears, folded paws, tail), never glued spheres, at real size (an adult cat curled ≈ 37 cm across, on furniture sized for it); colour in the coat's real pattern (tabby stripes over the back, pale chest and paws). Fur is shells: ~32 copies of the skin pushed out along the normal and, toward the tips, along the way the hair is combed (it lies down the body, it does not stand up), a fragment kept only where a strand passes — strands on the skin's own uv (a 3D grid sliced by the surface shows rings), jittered, at the species' real hair spacing and length (a short-haired cat: ~0.7 mm apart, 3–10 mm long), thinner and lighter toward the tip. Whiskers are fine tubes. Close-up check before the wide.
  - **Air moves light things** (`camera-motion.md` §5): a fan's jet or a breeze sways cloth, leaves and grass in its path, steam and smoke bend in it; nothing light stays still in moving air.
  - **Steam and smoke are seen by contrast.** Real steam shows when backlit or in front of something darker: white on a light sweep or a cream wall is invisible, and raising its alpha only makes a solid column. Frame the plume against a darker part of the set (a dark tile, a shadowed wall, the inside of a cupboard) or light it from the side or behind; check `p.contrast(groundColour).readable` for the colour behind it. Indoors the air is never still: add a room draught (`wind.js` `draught()`, ~0.1 m/s) so a plume from a cup, a pot, a chimney or a beaker breaks into wisps, unless the shot has a reason for none (a sealed chamber).
  - **A bulk of small things shows its grains.** Rice in a jar, coffee in a sack, lentils in a pot, sand in a pit, gravel on a path: separate grains at real size at the surface and at a cut (`engine/three/grains.js`, at least two jittered layers, each grain its own tone, plump ellipsoids or angular stones per material). A smooth slab with a noise texture reads as foam or gravel and capsules as pills; a slab stands in only where a grain is under ~3 px (`instances: false`, its surface carries the grain texture). A cut through loose grain shows whole grains, not sliced ones: pass the cutaway's `{ normal, offset }` to `grains()`; the cutaway then drives the fill (exterior shot, slid cut, rotated container), so one cut object controls the wall and the grains.
  - **Everything that moves is heard** (`camera-motion.md` §5): a running machine, a roll or a slide following its speed in the bake, each knock, rain, a fire.
  - **A light that is on lights what is around it**, falling off with the square of the distance, at its real colour temperature, with shadows, shaped by what holds it (e.g. a drum-shade lamp: a down cone pooling on the floor, an up cone scalloping the wall, a dim glow through the cloth, ~2700 K; a screen: a rect-area light its size; embers: a low orange point light).
  - **A display shows on the model's own screen face** (where the set has one): find the face by its material, lay the picture on its plane a hair in front, size the picture to its aspect; sticky notes and stickers sit on that plane and are attached to the model. Hand-typed coordinates left a picture floating centimetres in front of a monitor.
  - **A zero normal is a black frame:** a swept tube's closed tip has zero-length normals, `normalize()` gives NaN in the shader and bloom spreads it over the whole frame. Set those normals to the tube's axis.
  - **A cutaway (section view) cuts the whole assembly, with caps that never fight a face.** Use `engine/three/cutaway.js` and never a hand-written clipping plane: one plane on every part (parts built later too, through `cut.add`), because a part left whole overhangs the cut body and reads as a second piece (a piston left whole beside a cut cylinder); a cap lying exactly on a face under it z-fights into streaks, so every cap is one quad on one transform, pulled ahead by `polygonOffset`. A hollow thing shows its wall: model it with its inner and outer wall, or flag a single-surface part `userData.cut = { shell: metres }`, so the gap the narration names (an engine's cylinder wall and water jacket, a lock's cylinder and pins, a heart valve's leaflets, a water heater's tank and insulation, a pen's barrel and refill) can be seen and not a solid disc. The exterior shot and the section shot are the same set: `cut.off(t)` and `cut.on(t)`, a slow `cut.set(offset, t)` slides the section through. The renderer needs a stencil buffer. The render draws a clone of each material, so a glowing element, a fade or a valve changing colour keeps working when the set edits its own material and calls `cut.at(t)` once per frame (it carries the edits over; a shader's uniforms are shared).

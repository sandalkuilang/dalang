# Looks (Step 1 options; Step 5 reads only the chosen row)

Each look is a medium with numbers, not an adjective. A look decides everything around the subject (set, light, type, graphics, rhythm); every look is stylized and the subject stays true to reality (`visual-direction.md` §1): C, G, I, J, K draw or build it; D and H are type and data. Letters keep their old names, so a series kit stays readable. Write the chosen one as the LOOK line of `SPEC.md`. "Feels like" names a genre so the user can picture it; it is never a licence to copy anyone's characters, frames, or brand.

A preview is a promise: nothing from `visual-direction.md` §5 "Banned" and no void ground (`visual-direction.md` §9), or `SPEC.md` will have to break it.

## three.js recipes (verified: a 3 s 1080p24 shot renders in under 30 s on a 16 GB M5)

### Install the engine into the project

Install the DALANG engine into the project (`node <skill-dir>/engine/three/build.mjs assets`, then `node <skill-dir>/engine/three/selfcheck.mjs assets`; `<skill-dir>/engine/three/README.md`; the build lists every file it copied, with its real origin and licence, in `assets/.engine.json`, and `check_assets.mjs` reads that, so the engine never goes into `ASSETS.json` by hand): one local ESM bundle of three and its add-ons, so every module shares one `THREE`, and the proven systems for everything below (scale, surfaces, settling, machine parts and transmissions, electricity, fields, light, air jets, granular fills, section views, finish; ready pieces from earlier sets in `examples/`). Build on them. The engine holds what earlier films proved; it is not the limit of what a film can show. A subject it has nothing for (a cell's chemistry, a solar system, an organ, a market, an idea) is built the same way: find the law that drives it and its real numbers in a cited source; model it in real units from three.js primitives (instanced meshes, lines, shaders), or show it in a look that needs no 3D (H's data, D's type); when reality is too small, large, fast or slow to see, change one scale on purpose and show it (`camera-motion.md` §5, "Any subject moves by its own law"); self-check it against those numbers and prove it in a render; then bring it back to the engine as a generic system (its README, "how it grows"). Shaders compile on the first frames and timers are frozen in the capture browser: never wait on a timer in a build step.

### Light and material per look

- **Studio diorama (C).** The best-loved three.js scenes are Blender models with lighting baked into one texture; without Blender, approximate the bake: `RoomEnvironment` through PMREM as the environment (intensity ~0.9: soft, even studio light), `MeshStandardMaterial` roughness ~0.55, metalness 0, each part's sides a step darker; parts thicker and rounded (extrude bevel thickness ~15 % of depth, 5 segments; widen meshing parts' spacing by twice the bevel growth); a mount or housing with small details; a `ShadowMaterial` ground (opacity ~0.22) under one soft directional (PCF radius ~12); N8AO (`studio()` sets its radius); neutral tone mapping; no DOF; grain ≤ 0.12 and a vignette near the ground colour. The ground is light and warm (row C); the dark parts, N8AO and the `ShadowMaterial` contact shadow carry the separation: check ΔL with `palette.mjs --compare` on a style frame. A real bake (Blender, Cycles) is richer still — bounce light, glowing practicals — and works only for parts that do not move relative to each other.
- **Stylized (I):** `MeshToonMaterial` with a 3-texel ramp (nearest filtering); an ink outline as an inverted hull (`BackSide`, pushed out ~1 % of the object's size along normals smoothed with `mergeVertices`, so hard edges don't crack the line); no tone mapping, so the picked colours stay; key light plus hemisphere fill, shadow radius ≤ 2; no grain.
- **Technical clean (K):** a light warm seamless sweep (e.g. #ecebe6) with a `ShadowMaterial` contact shadow; `RoomEnvironment` through PMREM (~0.8) and one soft key with a PCF shadow; N8AO; neutral tone mapping; no grain, DOF only in a macro. Parts in `MeshStandardMaterial` greys by part type (~#2b2b2b to #d9d9d6, roughness 0.4–0.6, metal parts metalness ~0.6); the named part in the one accent. The housing in x-ray: `MeshPhysicalMaterial` transparent, opacity ~0.12–0.2, `depthWrite: false`, drawn after the parts inside, plus its silhouette edges (`EdgesGeometry`, ~30°) at ~0.35 opacity so its shape still reads. K skips set dressing, surface detail maps and condition (the "every 3D set" rules below), never real shapes, proportions, scale or physical motion.
- **Engineering cutaway (J):** a light-grey ground with a gridded mat; the example's `lights()` (key, fill, rim), or `studio()`'s; AgX tone mapping, N8AO radius ≈ framed radius / 12, grain 0.035, `BloomEffect` threshold 1.0 (only current, LEDs and arcs pass it); DOF on the macro station only (`frames().macro.dof` into `DepthOfFieldEffect`); hard-surface materials, the assembly cut with `cutaway.js` (below). Overlays: Text band (`textBand`): kicker in monospace caps, tracked, 36 px, over a bold headline with the claim or number (≈ 76 px), a dark status tag. Labels: ≤ 3 per frame, yellow pills on a dark leader ending in a ringed dot (`pinLabel` `pill`). Caption: dark pill, the key word in a yellow box. Accents mean one thing for the whole film: yellow labels and AC, blue DC, data or cold, orange heat, red warning.

### Set dressing, real scale, interior style, condition

- **Set dressing (every 3D set but K's; it decides whether C reads as real or as a toy).** The things around the subject, at their real density, carry the believability more than any light or material: a room sparser than a real one reads as a toy, cartoon or not; space stays nearly empty, the inside of a cell or a battery is crowded. Dress every surface with what would really be there in this place and show it was used (a desk: papers, a mug, cables to an outlet; walls: a pinboard, a clock, a switch; a lab bench: glassware, labels, a notebook; a workshop: offcuts, tools, sawdust; outdoors: leaf litter, pebbles, a puddle), plants with real leaf shapes (a `ShapeGeometry` blade, cupped, on stems; trailing vines) rather than cones, and, where life or machines are present, two or three small motions besides the hero, at their real amount (a breathing pet, a clock's tick, a blinking cursor, rising steam, bubbles in a beaker; still air indoors, steady LEDs). Anything that falls, rolls or is knocked is posed from its cause (`camera-motion.md` §5). Surfaces get canvas textures (plank seams and grain, a rug pattern, screen content, a view out of the window). CC0 kits give the props cheaply (for interiors Kenney Furniture Kit GLB and Quaternius furniture OBJ: `examples/interior.js` `kits.furniture` with a palette; other kits get their own name maps): recolour them by material name into the film's palette, re-shade with `toCreasedNormals` at ~32°, place by footprint centre (`model(path, { at: "footprint" })`; a kit whose origins sit where the piece stands may use them), and light the dressed set with a `CubeCamera` probe rendered once from its centre as `scene.environment` (bounce light without a bake). Work out pixels per real metre from the framing (frame width in px ÷ metres visible across it; a three-quarter view of a 4 m room in 1080p ≈ 150 px/m): a detail under ~6 px reads as noise, so build it only for close-ups. Keep the props in `assets/*.js` modules so the scene file stays under the lint's ~400 lines. A small light patch (sun through a small window) is lost behind props and spill: give a light a clear path or skip it.
- **Real scale first** (`kit.js` `makeKit({ mpu })`). Measure the set's metres per unit from things of known size (a desk top 0.75 m, a door 2.03 m, a car 4.5 m, a test tube 15 cm — kit models are rarely 1 unit = 1 m). Every new prop is modelled in metres and scaled by 1/M at its real size (an adult cat curled ≈ 37 cm, a hardcover 16 × 24 cm, an AA cell 50 × 14 mm, a 16-inch fan); physics runs in real units (the place's gravity over M units/s², 9.81 m/s² on Earth; speeds and drag converted), or everything falls too fast and the set reads as a miniature. Reports read in real mm and m/s.
- **Interior style (only for a set with rooms or furniture; from `BRIEF.md` `interior`; default American-Japandi).** Japanese calm with American home motifs: honey bamboo furniture (laminated strips with growth nodes) and light oak floors, warm white walls, a navy or white Shaker panel door with a white casing and brass knob, oatmeal linen upholstery, navy and a little rust as accents; cushions in ticking stripe, navy linen and buffalo check, a braided cotton rug, a rice-paper lamp shade, an ensō or a quiet print, a ceramic pot. Patterns are drawn as yarn (each band of threads, a twill or weft line), not flat paint.
- **Condition (from `BRIEF.md` `condition`; any made thing: a room, a machine, a street).** `lived-in` (default): sound walls (roller stipple and faint mottling), things in use; `new`: no traces of use; `worn`: flaking paint at damp spots and edges, scuffs where hands and feet go, grime low on walls. Never age a set the topic did not ask to be old.

### True shapes and true surfaces

- **True shapes and true surfaces (every 3D set, stylized too; K keeps the shapes and skips the surface detail: the shape and the stuff it is made of are real, the palette and framing may be stylized). Always, whatever the subject; each rule applies where its thing is in the set:**
  - **No facets, no blocks.** Low-poly kit models read as toy blocks. Soft and organic things (upholstery, cushions, leaves, a stuffed toy) get two Loop subdivisions (`three-subdivide`, MIT, coplanar faces split first so flat faces stay flat); round hard things (pots, vases) one; hard furniture none — a Loop pass melts a desk into clay, its crisp corners are what make it read as wood. Primitives at 64 segments, rounded boxes at 5.
  - **Every surface has its material's detail**, as tileable map pairs baked from seeded noise (albedo multiplier + under-layer mask, normal + roughness), laid by triplanar projection so no UVs are needed: walls of paint over plaster with a roller stipple (flaking where damp rises, each flake's edge curling with a line of dirt in the break, grime low down — only at the brief's `worn` condition); plain-weave cloth (1 mm threads, slub, fuzz) with `MeshPhysicalMaterial` sheen on upholstery, cushions and curtains; a cotton loop pile on rugs; fine, nearly straight wood grain (~4 mm lines — wide wavy rings read as a contour map); faint smudges in the gloss of everything else. Fixed set pieces project in world space (one tile spans neighbouring wall panels), moving bodies in their own space (the texture travels with them). `material.clone()` drops `onBeforeCompile`: copy it across.
  - **Soft things are stuffed and give under weight.** A cushion is fat in the middle and thin at its seam (a finely divided box, its thickness scaled by the distance from the edge — a rounded box's flat faces are single quads that cannot bulge); a pouf is a sewn drum with piping, its top domed and pressed down where something lies on it. Loose soft things (cushions, a dropped coat) are placed leaning against what is there and settled with `settle()`, at rest from frame 0.
  - **Every prop rests on its own lowest points.** Drop it by the smallest gap under its lowest vertex in each cell of a 6 × 6 grid over its footprint (legs, corners, a leaning edge), so it lands on its first contact; a point below a surface means it was placed into it (lift out up to 3 cm, deeper is reported). One ray under the centre drops a desk between its legs through the floor and leaves a leaning cushion hanging on a back edge. boxes from the vertices, not a rotated mesh's own box.
  - **Animals are built from their anatomy.** Sweep each part along its centre line with its real girth (spine, haunch, chest, neck, skull, ears, paws, tail), never glued spheres, at real size, coloured in the coat's real pattern (tabby stripes over the back, pale chest and paws); a lizard or an insect has scales or cuticle. An animal that moves does so briefly, its stride from its size (`camera-motion.md` §5); close-up check before the wide.
  - **Air moves light things** (`camera-motion.md` §5): a fan's jet leans a ribbon or a paper strip in its path; nothing light stays still in moving air.
  - **A bulk of small things shows its grains.** Rice in a jar, coffee in a sack, lentils in a pot, sand in a pit, gravel on a path: separate grains at real size at the surface and at a cut (`engine/three/grains.js`, at least two jittered layers, each grain its own tone, plump ellipsoids or angular stones per material). A smooth slab with a noise texture reads as foam or gravel and capsules as pills; a slab stands in only where a grain is under ~3 px (`instances: false`, its surface carries the grain texture). A cut through loose grain shows whole grains, not sliced ones: pass the cutaway's `{ normal, offset }` to `grains()`; the cutaway then drives the fill (exterior shot, slid cut, rotated container), so one cut object controls the wall and the grains.
  - **Everything that moves is heard** (`camera-motion.md` §5): a running machine, a roll or a slide following its speed, each knock.
  - **A light that is on lights what is around it**, falling off with the square of the distance, at its real colour temperature, with shadows, shaped by what holds it (e.g. a drum-shade lamp: a down cone pooling on the floor, an up cone scalloping the wall, a dim glow through the cloth, ~2700 K; a screen: a rect-area light its size; embers: a low orange point light).
  - **Made things carry their real markings** (`engine/three/markings.js`, `electronics.js`): ratings, model numbers, silkscreen, warning stickers and conductor colours at real size, legible at the macro (`glyphPx` >= `READ_PX`), the text from the film's sources: a phone charger opened, a toy's battery box, a kettle's base switch.
  - **A display shows on the model's own screen face** (where the set has one): find the face by its material, lay the picture on its plane a hair in front, size the picture to its aspect; sticky notes and stickers sit on that plane and are attached to the model. Hand-typed coordinates left a picture floating centimetres in front of a monitor.
  - **A zero normal is a black frame:** a swept tube's closed tip has zero-length normals, `normalize()` gives NaN in the shader and bloom spreads it over the whole frame. Set those normals to the tube's axis.
  - **A cutaway (section view) cuts the whole assembly, with caps that never fight a face.** Use `engine/three/cutaway.js` and never a hand-written clipping plane: one plane on every part (parts built later too, through `cut.add`), because a part left whole overhangs the cut body and reads as a second piece (a piston left whole beside a cut cylinder); a cap lying exactly on a face under it z-fights into streaks, so every cap is one quad on one transform, pulled ahead by `polygonOffset`. A hollow thing shows its wall: model it with its inner and outer wall, or flag a single-surface part `userData.cut = { shell: metres }`, so the gap the narration names (an engine's cylinder wall and water jacket, a lock's cylinder and pins, a heart valve's leaflets, a water heater's tank and insulation, a pen's barrel and refill) can be seen and not a solid disc. The exterior shot and the section shot are the same set: `cut.off(t)` and `cut.on(t)`, a slow `cut.set(offset, t)` slides the section through. The renderer needs a stencil buffer. The render draws a clone of each material, so a glowing element, a fade or a valve changing colour keeps working when the set edits its own material and calls `cut.at(t)` once per frame (it carries the edits over; a shader's uniforms are shared).

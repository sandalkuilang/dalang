# The looks: isometric-world first, cutaway, blueprint (Step 5 reads this)

Two looks, either mode, picked in the Brief (`brief.md` Call 1 item 0b): the isometric family and `object-studio` (last section). The isometric family is three EpicInfographics languages (`engine/epic/languages/`, MIT), always isometric, flat drawing read as 3D (2.5D) through layers and colour: **`isometric-world`**, the main language of every film: the subject as a small world on a floating slab with a cut edge, empty pale sky, the data built into the world; **`cutaway`** on the beats that open a thing up: the same view on paper, the subject sliced open, tinted compartments, ink lines, numbered callouts (the numbers of the script's parts table, `script-voice.md` "Anatomy explainer"; a number stays on its part all film); **`blueprint` only on the beats that need engineering-grade detail** (many exact measures, dimensions or tolerances to read): near-white linework on blueprint blue, dimension lines with real values, a title block. `BRIEF.md` `look:` and `SPEC.md` LOOK name the main language, the beats using the other two, the composition pattern, the metaphor and the palette slots. A request for a look outside the Brief's two is answered in one line; its palette and type may go into the chosen look, never its projection. The subject stays true to reality (`visual-direction.md` §1).

| | Express (`express.md`) | Studio (below) |
|---|---|---|
| Drawn by | `engine/iso` (Canvas 2D) for `isometric-world` and `cutaway`; inline SVG for `blueprint` | `engine/three`: `isoCamera`, `isoStudio`; `isoLook` (world), `isoLook` + `inkLines` (cutaway), `blueprintLook` + `inkLines` (blueprint) |
| Projection | true isometric; the view pans and zooms (zoom in log), never tilts or turns (a turn is the subject's); depth from layers moved against each other | the same: an `OrthographicCamera` along (−1, −1, −1), a pose `{ target, k }` |
| Faces | `isometric-world`: three tones from one colour, each solid inked (silhouette + crease, `iso.ink`); `cutaway`: paper tints, ink lines; `blueprint`: lines only | tones from the face's normal (one shared matcap) or flat ground-coloured faces, no lights |
| Palette, type, furniture | the language file: ground, slots, ink, type scale; pins or numbered callouts, route lines, dashed cut lines, hatched sections, Isotype rows, insets, dimension lines; a title block on 16:9 only | the same, on the 2D canvas over the set |
| Depth and finish | `engine/iso/parallax.js` layers, `cinema.js` finish | the same modules, the three.js canvas as the subject layer |
| Studio only | — | the Blender hero (`hero-asset.md`), section views (`cutaway.js`), grains, fields, current, posed motion, reference boards, benchmark, reviewer |

**Format.** Every language size (type, line weights, pins) is for a 1080 px short side, scaled by min(W, H) / 1080; the frame sets the frustum (`isoCamera(W, H)`). 9:16: headline in the top ~18 %, caption zone clear, the subject in the middle band at ≥ 1/3 of the height at key moments, a story path (road, pipe) climbing the frame.

## three.js recipes (Studio)

### Install the engine into the project

`node <skill-dir>/engine/three/build.mjs assets`, then `node <skill-dir>/engine/three/selfcheck.mjs assets` (`<skill-dir>/engine/three/README.md`). One local ESM bundle (one shared `THREE`) with the proven systems: scale, settling, electricity, fields, light, air jets, granular fills, section views, finish; each film builds its own objects from them. The build lists every copied file with origin and licence in `assets/.engine.json`, which `check_assets.mjs` reads: never put the engine into `ASSETS.json`. A subject the engine has nothing for: find its law and real numbers in a cited source, model it in real units from three.js primitives (instanced meshes, lines, shaders) or as data furniture, change one scale on purpose and show it when reality can't be seen (`camera-motion.md` §5.4), self-check it against the numbers, prove it in a render, then add it to the engine as a generic system (its README). Timers are frozen in the capture browser: never wait on a timer in a build step.

### The iso look in three.js

- **Camera:** `isoCamera(W, H)` (`camera.js`): frustum in px, so `k` is px per unit (as `engine/iso`'s `cam.k`). Stations are `isoFrame(object, { W, H, margin })` → `{ target, k }`; `stations()`, `route()`, `travel()` and `shots.js` take them with no `lift`: the target pans, `k` zooms in log, the direction never changes. `orbit`, `crane`, `dollyZoom`, `drone`, `fovFromMm`, `dofFor` are for `object-studio` only.
- **Surfaces:** `isoLook(root)` (`studio.js`) after the set is built, before `cutaway()` and `attend()`: every mesh onto one `MeshMatcapMaterial` per original, colour = the triad's **top** tone; the matcap gives left (+z) ×0.8, right (+x) ×0.62 and blends slanted faces (`ISO_TONES`, `isoTone`). Colour, map, opacity and clipping are kept. An emissive part keeps its own material (its glow is the subject, `circuit.js`).
- **Rig:** `isoStudio(renderer, scene, camera, { ground: "#E8EDEF", finish })`: no lights, shadows, AO, tone mapping or bloom; `render(t, pose)`; `finish` = `cinema.js` `makeFinish(...).apply` (grade, vignette, grain 0.04–0.06).
- **`cutaway`:** `isoLook` with the language's slots as top tones (compartments at 30–55 % over paper `#FAF6EC`), `isoStudio({ ground: "#FAF6EC" })`, `cutaway(root, { flat: true, hatch })` (unlit hatched caps, the cut face a step warmer), then `inkLines(root, { color: "#2B2A26" })` after the cut. Numbered callouts, a detail lens, tiny figures for scale.
- **`blueprint`:** `blueprintLook(root, { bg: "#123A66" })` (faces flat in the ground colour, so nearer parts hide lines behind), then `inkLines(root, { color: "#D8E8F8" })`; a mesh flagged `userData.data` keeps its chart-slot colour. `isoStudio({ ground: "#123A66" })`; the 40 px grid, `dimension` lines with real values and the title block on the 2D canvas.
- **The slab (`isometric-world`):** the set on a floating slab (`--ground` top, `--ground-2` sides), a visible cut edge, nothing under it; the empty sky is the breathing zone.
- **The place around it (`isometric-world`, Express):** the slab is the subject's real surroundings, never a bare plate: the ground carries its finish (a kitchen's `tile`, a room's `planks`, a street's `asphalt`, a yard's `grass` over `soil`, a workshop's `concrete`) and the side layers theirs, dressed with what really stands there at its real density (a workbench and its tools beside a motor, a socket and cable beside an appliance, kerb, drain and a plant pot beside a pump). The surroundings stay calmer than the subject (ground tones, the subject keeps the accent).
- **Electronics read as the real parts:** a board is a `pcb` with its copper traces and tinned pads, its parts at datasheet size (`resistor` with its colour bands, `diode` with its cathode band, `elco` with its sleeve, minus stripe and scored top, `ceramic`, `smd` chips, `ic` DIP or SOIC with its legs and pin-1 dot, a `transformer` with its laminated core and copper winding), each drawn in the isometric look; never a coloured block or a generic chip.
- **Mechanical parts carry their surface:** every made part names its `finish` (`brushed` drawn metal, `knurl` on a grip, `cast` iron or aluminium, `rubber`, `wood`, `weave`), laid in the face's own plane so it turns with the part; painted, glazed and glossy plastic stay plain.
- **Furniture:** `pinLabel`, `dimension`, `drawCaption`, `textBand` on the 2D canvas in the language's ink and the one typeface; pins never cover the subject; numbers as Isotype rows where they can be.

### Set dressing, real scale, interior style, condition

- **Set dressing (every set):** the things around the subject at their real density decide real vs toy (a room sparser than real reads as a toy; space is nearly empty, a cell crowded). Dress every surface with what is really there, used (a desk: papers, a mug, cables to an outlet; a lab bench: glassware, labels; a workshop: offcuts, sawdust); plants with real leaf shapes (`ShapeGeometry` blades on stems), never cones; where life or machines are present, two or three small motions besides the hero at their real amount (a clock's tick, rising steam). Surfaces carry their pattern as drawn detail in the triad's tones. CC0 kits for props (interiors: Kenney Furniture Kit GLB, Quaternius furniture OBJ): recolour by material name into the triads, re-shade with `toCreasedNormals` at ~32°, place by footprint centre (`model(path, { at: "footprint" })`). Pixels per metre = frame width px ÷ metres across (a 4 m room in 1080p ≈ 150 px/m); a detail under ~6 px only for close-ups. Props in `assets/*.js` modules so the scene file stays under ~400 lines.
- **Real scale first** (`kit.js` `makeKit({ mpu })`): measure metres per unit from known sizes (desk top 0.75 m, door 2.03 m, car 4.5 m); model every prop in metres, scaled by 1/M (a curled cat ≈ 37 cm, an AA cell 50 × 14 mm); motion in real units (`camera-motion.md` §5.2). Report in mm and m/s.
- **Interior style** (sets with rooms or furniture; `BRIEF.md` `interior`, default American-Japandi): Japanese calm with American home motifs, coloured from the triads: bamboo furniture with nodes, plank floors, a Shaker panel door, linen upholstery, ticking-stripe and buffalo-check cushions, a braided rug, a rice-paper shade, a ceramic pot; patterns as bands of the triad's tones.
- **Condition** (`BRIEF.md` `condition`, any made thing): `lived-in` (default) in use; `new` no traces; `worn` flaking paint at damp spots and edges, scuffs where hands go, grime low on walls, in the next darker tone. Never age a set the topic did not ask for.

### True shapes and true surfaces

Shape and material are real, palette and projection the language's. Each rule applies where its thing is in the set:
- **No facets:** soft and organic things get two Loop subdivisions (`three-subdivide`, MIT, coplanar faces split first), round hard things one, hard furniture none; primitives at 64 segments, rounded boxes at 5.
- **Material by drawn pattern** (no lit maps): plank seams and grain, cloth weave as thread bands, a rug's loop pile, plaster stipple, flaking only at `worn`. Fixed pieces carry pattern in world space, moving bodies in their own.
- **Soft things are stuffed and give:** a cushion fat in the middle, thin at the seam (a finely divided box scaled by distance from the edge); a pouf domed and pressed where something lies. Loose soft things lean on what is there and are `settle()`d, at rest from frame 0.
- **Every prop rests on its own lowest points** (`camera-motion.md` §5.4 `settle`; boxes from vertices, not a rotated mesh's box).
- **Animals from their anatomy:** each part swept along its centre line with real girth (spine, haunch, chest, neck, skull, ears, paws, tail), never glued spheres, in the coat's real pattern; scales or cuticle where true. Movement brief, stride from size; close-up check before the wide.
- **Air moves light things** and **everything that moves is heard** (`camera-motion.md` §5.4).
- **Bulk of small things shows its grains** (`engine/three/grains.js`): separate grains at real size at the surface and at a cut, ≥ 2 jittered layers, each grain its own tone, shape per material; a slab only where a grain is under ~3 px (`instances: false`). A cut shows whole grains: pass the cutaway's `{ normal, offset }` to `grains()`, so one cut drives wall and grains.
- **A light that is on shows it:** the lamp in its emissive colour at its colour temperature, its pool drawn as a paler patch shaped by its holder.
- **Made things carry real markings** (`hero-asset.md`): ratings, model numbers, silkscreen, stickers, conductor colours at real size, cap height ≥ 12 px at the macro, text from the sources.
- **A display shows on the model's own screen face:** find it by material, lay the picture on its plane a hair in front at its aspect; stickers on that plane, attached to the model.
- **A zero normal is a black frame:** set a swept tube's closed-tip normals to its axis.
- **A cutaway cuts the whole assembly** (`engine/three/cutaway.js`, never a hand clipping plane): one plane on every part (later parts via `cut.add`); each cap one quad pulled ahead by `polygonOffset`; a hollow thing shows its wall (model inner and outer wall, or `userData.cut = { shell: metres }`). Exterior and section are the same set (`cut.off(t)`, `cut.on(t)`, `cut.set(offset, t)`). Needs a stencil buffer. Call `cut.at(t)` once per frame when the set edits its own materials (it carries the edits to the clones).

## object-studio (3D, Express or Studio)

The real object as a clean true-to-life CG render: stylized, never photoreal, real scale, `engine/three`, one look per film.
- **Set:** one light grey sweep (floor `#D9D9D6` curving to wall `#ECECEA`), `studio()` light with AO and contact shadows. Perspective camera: 3/4 views, eye level and low, macro inserts with shallow focus (`camera-motion.md`).
- **X-ray:** the shell turns semi-transparent cool grey (`attend` with `edge`, `cutaway.js`); the named part takes one highlight colour.
- **Overlays:** a mono caps kicker and a bold headline with one accent word on a brand-colour chip, over a gradient glass band (the picture, then a frosted band over the top 27 % fading downward, shown with the headline, then the text; no band at the bottom; the glass tinted from the ground behind it, white only on a dark ground such as blueprint, with a fixed frosted grain and a light lip where it starts to fade); white data cards with a source line; pill labels with leaders; hanging tags for A vs B; karaoke captions, the active word on the accent chip. Key content out of the bottom 22 %, captions aside. One language and casing for every overlay.
- **Close and x-ray stay recognisable:** the object's silhouette (or an inset of it) stays in frame; the explained part fills ≤ half the frame and the mechanism's arrows land inside it. A mirror (`mat.chrome`) needs contrast in its view.
- **Subject box (9:16):** the subject and all it does between 18 % and 60 % of the height (y 345–1150 on 1080×1920), above the caption band (`editorial.js` `safe.bottom`, 1180); a wide shows the object at ~40 % of the height; a fix never moves it below the box.
- **Aperture:** wide f5.6, zoom-in or macro f1.2–2.8 (`cam(..., { dof: { fstop, focus } })`).
- **Material effects get two snapshot rounds;** one that still doesn't read is logged and left.
- **Figure-ground:** ≥ 25 L* between the hero and what is right behind it (a dark rim, a contact shadow, a darker backdrop).
- **Shared modules, no pilot:** the object-studio kit (`engine/three/README.md` "object-studio kit") imported unchanged by every group.
- **Clear things:** a liquid uses the kit's liquid material (plain alpha); clear plastic and glass its additive reflection-only materials.
- **Banned:** isometric slabs, grid paper, title blocks, retro print; brands and logos; footage and generated images.

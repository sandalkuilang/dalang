# The looks: isometric-world first, cutaway, blueprint (Step 5 reads this)

Two looks, either mode, picked in the Brief (`brief.md` Call 1 item 0b): the isometric family below and `object-studio` (its own section at the end). The isometric family is three design languages from EpicInfographics (`engine/epic/languages/`, MIT), isometric always, flat drawing made to read as 3D (2.5D) through layers and colour: **`isometric-world`** is the main one, the subject as a small world on a floating slab with a cut edge, empty pale sky around it, the data built into the world; **`cutaway`** is its close relative, the same view on paper with the subject sliced open, hand-tinted compartments, ink linework and numbered callouts; **`blueprint`** is for very detailed data, near-white linework on blueprint blue with dimension lines carrying real values and a title block. `isometric-world` is the main language of every film; `cutaway` joins it on the beats that open a thing up; `blueprint` only on the beats that need engineering-grade detail (many exact measures, dimensions, specs or tolerances the viewer must read). `BRIEF.md` `look:` and the LOOK line of `SPEC.md` name the main language, the beats that use the other two, the composition pattern, the metaphor and the palette slots used. A request for a look outside the Brief's two is answered in one line; its palette and type go into the chosen look, never its projection. Every look is stylized and the subject stays true to reality (`visual-direction.md` §1).

| | Express (`express.md`) | Studio (below) |
|---|---|---|
| Drawn by | `engine/iso` (Canvas 2D) for `isometric-world` and `cutaway`; inline SVG for `blueprint` | `engine/three` (three.js): `isoCamera`, `isoStudio`; `isoLook` (world), `isoLook` + `inkLines` (cutaway), `blueprintLook` + `inkLines` (blueprint) |
| Projection | true isometric; the view pans and zooms (zoom in log), never tilts or turns (a turn is the subject's, never the camera's); depth from layers moved against each other (2.5D) | the same: an `OrthographicCamera` looking along (−1, −1, −1), a pose `{ target, k }` |
| Faces | `isometric-world`: three tones from one colour, no outlines; `cutaway`: paper tints, ink lines; `blueprint`: lines only | the same, from the face's normal (one shared matcap) or flat ground-coloured faces, no lights |
| Palette, type, furniture | the language file: its ground, slots, ink and fonts; label pins or numbered callouts, route lines, dashed cut lines, hatched section faces, Isotype rows, insets, dimension lines; a title block only on 16:9, never on 9:16 or 4:5 (sheet furniture reads as coursework) | the same file, the same furniture on the 2D canvas over the set |
| Depth and finish | `engine/iso/parallax.js` layers, `cinema.js` finish | the same modules, the three.js canvas as the subject layer |
| What only Studio adds | — | real parts and transmissions, section views (`cutaway.js`), grains, fields, current, motion posed from its cause, reference boards, benchmark, reviewer |

**Format.** Wide (16:9) and story (9:16) alike: the frame decides the frustum (`isoCamera(W, H)`), and every size of the language (type, line weights, pins) is for a 1080 px short side, scaled by min(W, H) / 1080. In 9:16 the headline sits in the top ~18 %, the caption zone at the bottom stays clear, the subject fills the middle band at ≥ 1/3 of the frame height at its key moments, and a story path (road, pipe, route) climbs the frame instead of running across it.

## three.js recipes (Studio)

### Install the engine into the project

Install the DALANG engine into the project (`node <skill-dir>/engine/three/build.mjs assets`, then `node <skill-dir>/engine/three/selfcheck.mjs assets`; `<skill-dir>/engine/three/README.md`; the build lists every file it copied, with its real origin and licence, in `assets/.engine.json`, and `check_assets.mjs` reads that, so the engine never goes into `ASSETS.json` by hand): one local ESM bundle of three and its add-ons, so every module shares one `THREE`, and the proven systems for everything below (scale, surfaces, settling, machine parts and transmissions, electricity, fields, light, air jets, granular fills, section views, finish; ready pieces from earlier sets in `examples/`). Build on them. The engine holds what earlier films proved; it is not the limit of what a film can show. A subject it has nothing for (a cell's chemistry, a solar system, an organ, a market, an idea) is built the same way: find the law that drives it and its real numbers in a cited source; model it in real units from three.js primitives (instanced meshes, lines, shaders), or show it as the world's data furniture (Isotype rows, a chart on the slab); when reality is too small, large, fast or slow to see, change one scale on purpose and show it (`camera-motion.md` §5, "Any subject moves by its own law"); self-check it against those numbers and prove it in a render; then bring it back to the engine as a generic system (its README, "how it grows"). Shaders compile on the first frames and timers are frozen in the capture browser: never wait on a timer in a build step.

### The iso look in three.js

- **Camera:** `isoCamera(W, H)` (`camera.js`): its frustum is the frame in px, so `k` is px per scene unit, like `engine/iso`'s `cam.k`. Stations are `isoFrame(object, { W, H, margin })` → `{ target, k }`; `stations()`, `route()`, `travel()` and `shots.js` take them as they take perspective ones, with no `lift`: the target pans, `k` zooms in log, the direction never changes. The perspective helpers in `camera.js` (`orbit`, `crane`, `dollyZoom`, `drone`, `fovFromMm`, `dofFor`) serve `object-studio`, never an isometric film.
- **Surfaces:** `isoLook(root)` (`studio.js`) after the set is built and before `cutaway()` and `attend()`: every mesh onto one `MeshMatcapMaterial` per original, its colour the triad's **top** tone; the shared matcap gives left (+z) ×0.8 and right (+x) ×0.62 and blends a slanted face by its normal (`ISO_TONES`, `isoTone`). Colour, map, opacity and clipping are kept, so a cutaway still cuts and `attend()` still greys everything but the subject (focus + context). A glowing part (emissive) keeps its own material: unlit, it shows its glow, which is the subject (`circuit.js`).
- **Rig:** `isoStudio(renderer, scene, camera, { ground: "#E8EDEF", finish })`: no lights, no shadow map, no AO, no tone mapping, no bloom; `render(t, pose)` places the camera and draws; `finish` is `engine/iso/cinema.js`'s `makeFinish(...).apply` (grade, vignette, grain 0.04–0.06). A section is `cutaway(root, { flat: true, hatch })`: unlit caps, hatched like the language's section faces, the cut face a step warmer than the outside.
- **`cutaway` in three.js:** `isoLook` with the language's slots as top tones (compartments a slot at 30–55 % over the paper `#FAF6EC`, the cut face a step warmer), `isoStudio({ ground: "#FAF6EC" })`, the section by `cutaway(root, { flat: true, hatch })`, then `inkLines(root, { color: "#2B2A26" })` after the cut (the lines share its planes). Numbered callouts, a detail lens, tiny figures for scale.
- **`blueprint` in three.js:** `blueprintLook(root, { bg: "#123A66" })` (faces flat in the ground colour, so a nearer part hides the lines behind), then `inkLines(root, { color: "#D8E8F8" })`; a mesh flagged `userData.data` keeps its chart-slot colour (data marks never in the line colour). `isoStudio({ ground: "#123A66" })`; the 40 px grid, dimension lines (`dimension`) with real values, the title block on the 2D canvas.
- **The slab (`isometric-world`):** the set stands on a floating slab (language: `--ground` top, `--ground-2` sides) with a visible cut edge and nothing under it; the sky is empty and is the breathing zone.
- **Furniture on the frame:** `pinLabel`, `dimension`, `drawCaption`, `textBand` on the 2D canvas over the set in the language's ink and fonts; label pins never cover the subject; numbers drawn as Isotype rows where they can be.

### Set dressing, real scale, interior style, condition

- **Set dressing (every set; it decides whether the miniature reads as real or as a toy).** The things around the subject, at their real density, carry the believability more than any light or material: a room sparser than a real one reads as a toy; space stays nearly empty, the inside of a cell or a battery is crowded. Dress every surface with what would really be there in this place and show it was used (a desk: papers, a mug, cables to an outlet; walls: a pinboard, a clock, a switch; a lab bench: glassware, labels, a notebook; a workshop: offcuts, tools, sawdust; outdoors: leaf litter, pebbles, a puddle), plants with real leaf shapes (a `ShapeGeometry` blade, cupped, on stems; trailing vines) rather than cones, and, where life or machines are present, two or three small motions besides the hero, at their real amount (a breathing pet, a clock's tick, a blinking cursor, rising steam, bubbles in a beaker; still air indoors, steady LEDs). Anything that falls, rolls or is knocked is posed from its cause (`camera-motion.md` §5). Surfaces get their pattern as drawn detail in the triad's tones (plank seams, a rug's bands, screen content, a view out of the window). CC0 kits give the props cheaply (for interiors Kenney Furniture Kit GLB and Quaternius furniture OBJ: `examples/interior.js` `kits.furniture` with a palette; other kits get their own name maps): recolour them by material name into the language's triads (top tones), re-shade with `toCreasedNormals` at ~32° so each flat face takes one tone, and place by footprint centre (`model(path, { at: "footprint" })`; a kit whose origins sit where the piece stands may use them). Work out pixels per real metre from the framing (frame width in px ÷ metres visible across it; a three-quarter view of a 4 m room in 1080p ≈ 150 px/m): a detail under ~6 px reads as noise, so build it only for close-ups. Keep the props in `assets/*.js` modules so the scene file stays under the lint's ~400 lines. 
- **Real scale first** (`kit.js` `makeKit({ mpu })`). Measure the set's metres per unit from things of known size (a desk top 0.75 m, a door 2.03 m, a car 4.5 m, a test tube 15 cm — kit models are rarely 1 unit = 1 m). Every new prop is modelled in metres and scaled by 1/M at its real size (an adult cat curled ≈ 37 cm, a hardcover 16 × 24 cm, an AA cell 50 × 14 mm, a 16-inch fan); motion runs in real units (the place's gravity over M units/s², 9.81 m/s² on Earth; speeds and drag converted), or everything falls too fast and the set reads as a miniature. Reports read in real mm and m/s.
- **Interior style (only for a set with rooms or furniture; from `BRIEF.md` `interior`; default American-Japandi).** Japanese calm with American home motifs, in forms and patterns, coloured from the language's triads: bamboo furniture with growth nodes and plank floors, a Shaker panel door with its casing and knob, linen upholstery; cushions in ticking stripe and buffalo check, a braided rug, a rice-paper lamp shade, a quiet print, a ceramic pot. Patterns are drawn as bands of the triad's tones, not flat paint.
- **Condition (from `BRIEF.md` `condition`; any made thing: a room, a machine, a street).** `lived-in` (default): things in use; `new`: no traces of use; `worn`: flaking paint at damp spots and edges, scuffs where hands and feet go, grime low on walls, drawn as small shapes in the next darker tone. Never age a set the topic did not ask to be old.

### True shapes and true surfaces

- **True shapes and true surfaces (every set: the shape and the stuff it is made of are real, the palette and the projection are the language's). Always, whatever the subject; each rule applies where its thing is in the set:**
  - **No facets, no blocks.** Low-poly kit models read as toy blocks. Soft and organic things (upholstery, cushions, leaves, a stuffed toy) get two Loop subdivisions (`three-subdivide`, MIT, coplanar faces split first so flat faces stay flat); round hard things (pots, vases) one; hard furniture none — a Loop pass melts a desk into clay, its crisp corners are what make it read as wood. Primitives at 64 segments, rounded boxes at 5.
  - **Every surface shows its material, drawn:** no lit maps (the look has no lights), so the material reads through its pattern in the triad's tones and its true form: plank seams and fine straight grain lines, the weave of cloth as bands of threads, a rug's loop pile, the stipple of a plastered wall, a flaking edge where damp rises (only at `worn`). Fixed set pieces carry their pattern in world space (one pattern spans neighbouring panels), moving bodies in their own (it travels with them).
  - **Soft things are stuffed and give under weight.** A cushion is fat in the middle and thin at its seam (a finely divided box, its thickness scaled by the distance from the edge — a rounded box's flat faces are single quads that cannot bulge); a pouf is a sewn drum with piping, its top domed and pressed down where something lies on it. Loose soft things (cushions, a dropped coat) are placed leaning against what is there and settled with `settle()`, at rest from frame 0.
  - **Every prop rests on its own lowest points.** Drop it by the smallest gap under its lowest vertex in each cell of a 6 × 6 grid over its footprint (legs, corners, a leaning edge), so it lands on its first contact; a point below a surface means it was placed into it (lift out up to 3 cm, deeper is reported). One ray under the centre drops a desk between its legs through the floor and leaves a leaning cushion hanging on a back edge. boxes from the vertices, not a rotated mesh's own box.
  - **Animals are built from their anatomy.** Sweep each part along its centre line with its real girth (spine, haunch, chest, neck, skull, ears, paws, tail), never glued spheres, at real size, coloured in the coat's real pattern (tabby stripes over the back, pale chest and paws); a lizard or an insect has scales or cuticle. An animal that moves does so briefly, its stride from its size (`camera-motion.md` §5); close-up check before the wide.
  - **Air moves light things** (`camera-motion.md` §5): a fan's jet leans a ribbon or a paper strip in its path; nothing light stays still in moving air.
  - **A bulk of small things shows its grains.** Rice in a jar, coffee in a sack, lentils in a pot, sand in a pit, gravel on a path: separate grains at real size at the surface and at a cut (`engine/three/grains.js`, at least two jittered layers, each grain its own tone, plump ellipsoids or angular stones per material). A smooth slab with a noise texture reads as foam or gravel and capsules as pills; a slab stands in only where a grain is under ~3 px (`instances: false`, its surface carries the grain texture). A cut through loose grain shows whole grains, not sliced ones: pass the cutaway's `{ normal, offset }` to `grains()`; the cutaway then drives the fill (exterior shot, slid cut, rotated container), so one cut object controls the wall and the grains.
  - **Everything that moves is heard** (`camera-motion.md` §5): a running machine, a roll or a slide following its speed, each knock.
  - **A light that is on shows it:** the lamp itself in its emissive colour at its real colour temperature (a warm ~2700 K bulb, a cool screen), and the pool it casts drawn as a paler patch on what it lights, shaped by what holds it (a drum shade: a pool on the floor and a scallop on the wall).
  - **Made things carry their real markings** (`engine/three/markings.js`, `electronics.js`): ratings, model numbers, silkscreen, warning stickers and conductor colours at real size, legible at the macro (`glyphPx` >= `READ_PX`), the text from the film's sources: a phone charger opened, a toy's battery box, a kettle's base switch.
  - **A display shows on the model's own screen face** (where the set has one): find the face by its material, lay the picture on its plane a hair in front, size the picture to its aspect; sticky notes and stickers sit on that plane and are attached to the model. Hand-typed coordinates left a picture floating centimetres in front of a monitor.
  - **A zero normal is a black frame:** a swept tube's closed tip has zero-length normals, `normalize()` gives NaN in the shader and bloom spreads it over the whole frame. Set those normals to the tube's axis.
  - **A cutaway (section view) cuts the whole assembly, with caps that never fight a face.** Use `engine/three/cutaway.js` and never a hand-written clipping plane: one plane on every part (parts built later too, through `cut.add`), because a part left whole overhangs the cut body and reads as a second piece (a piston left whole beside a cut cylinder); a cap lying exactly on a face under it z-fights into streaks, so every cap is one quad on one transform, pulled ahead by `polygonOffset`. A hollow thing shows its wall: model it with its inner and outer wall, or flag a single-surface part `userData.cut = { shell: metres }`, so the gap the narration names (an engine's cylinder wall and water jacket, a lock's cylinder and pins, a heart valve's leaflets, a water heater's tank and insulation, a pen's barrel and refill) can be seen and not a solid disc. The exterior shot and the section shot are the same set: `cut.off(t)` and `cut.on(t)`, a slow `cut.set(offset, t)` slides the section through. The renderer needs a stencil buffer. The render draws a clone of each material, so a glowing element, a fade or a valve changing colour keeps working when the set edits its own material and calls `cut.at(t)` once per frame (it carries the edits over; a shader's uniforms are shared).


## object-studio (3D, Express or Studio)

The real object as a clean, true-to-life CG render: stylized (principle 6), never photoreal, true shapes at real scale. One look for the whole film, built with `engine/three`.
- **Set:** one light grey studio sweep (floor #D9D9D6 curving up to wall #ECECEA), `studio()` light with AO and contact shadows. A perspective camera: 3/4 views, eye level and low, macro inserts with shallow focus (`fovFromMm`, `dofFor`, `orbit`, `crane`, `dollyZoom`, `drone`; the plan in `camera-motion.md`).
- **X-ray:** going inside, the shell turns semi-transparent cool grey (`attend` with `edge`, `cutaway.js`) and the part the narration names takes one highlight colour.
- **Overlays:** a mono caps kicker and a bold headline with one accent word on a chip in the brand colour; white data cards with a source line; pill labels with a leader line to the part; an "illustration" chip while the picture is not measured; hanging tags for A vs B; karaoke captions with the active word on the accent chip. Key content stays out of the bottom 22 %, captions aside.
- **Macro and x-ray stay recognisable:** every close or x-ray shot keeps the object's silhouette in frame (or an inset of the whole object); the part it explains fills at most half the frame, and the arrows or particles of the mechanism land inside it, legible at phone size. A mirror (`mat.chrome`) reads only with contrast in its view.
- **Subject box (9:16):** the subject and everything it does (arrows, particles, a lifted lid) sit between 18 % and 60 % of the frame height (y 345–1150 on 1080×1920), above the caption band (editorial.js `safe.bottom`, 1180); headline above, cards beside the subject, never over it. A wide shows the whole object at about 40 % of the height. A fix that moves the subject never takes it below the box.
- **Aperture:** a wide at f5.6, a zoom-in or macro at f1.2–2.8 so the background blurs as that lens would (`cam(..., { dof: { fstop, focus } })`).
- **Material effects get two passes:** a material that does not read after two snapshot rounds (a mirror, a glow, a translucency) is logged in the report and left, never chased further.
- **One overlay voice:** every headline, label and the illustration chip in the narration language and one casing.
- **Figure-ground:** a coloured hero separates from the light sweep by value: a dark rim, a contact shadow, a soft darker backdrop behind it; at least 25 L* between the hero and what is right behind it.
- **Shared modules, no pilot:** the engine's object-studio kit (`engine/three/README.md` "object-studio kit": the set rig and the overlay system) is imported unchanged by every group.
- **Clear things:** a liquid uses the kit's liquid material (plain alpha; the transmission pass renders it opaque), clear plastic and glass its additive, reflection-only materials (an alpha-blended shell washes the colour inside out).
- **Banned:** isometric slabs, grid paper, title blocks, retro print; brands and logos; footage and generated images.
